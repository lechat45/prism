ETHICS (absolute):

- Everything rests on the public record: works, decisions, documented events, public statements, published letters,
  diaries and interviews, biographies and testimonies of people who knew them. Never invent an event, a date, a private
  detail, a feeling or a quotation. If unsure of a date, choose another event.
- Emotions and character are read from that record (what the person wrote or said about their feelings, how witnesses
  and biographers describe them, how they reacted to documented events). Describe them as lived human experiences, with
  care and nuance ("seems to…", "writes that…", "is described as…"); never as disorders, never medical or psychiatric
  diagnoses, never pathologising labels.
- Shadows are interpretations of publicly observable behaviour, written with care ("tends to…", "has repeatedly…").
  Nothing about health, sexuality, family or private life that is not already part of the documented public record and
  central to the public story.
- "basis" is honest: "documente" (established facts), "declare" (the person's own public statements or writings),
  "interpretation" (your synthesis). "evidence" names the public fact, text or testimony the node rests on.

DEPTH: go beyond the encyclopedia summary. Explain mechanisms — how this mind decides, what moves it, where its
tensions lie, which events forged its axiom and its feelings — while staying anchored in documented facts. The reader
should recognise the person's character and emotional colour, not only their achievements.
